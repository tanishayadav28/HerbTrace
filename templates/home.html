How a batch travels

Six roles each add one part of the record. A batch cannot skip a step: a failed laboratory test stops it for good.

1

Collector registers the harvest

Herb, quantity, method, source and permit, with the GPS position captured by the phone or entered by hand. A unique Batch ID is issued.

2

Region is checked

The position is compared with admin-approved regions. Outside any of them, the batch waits for an administrator's decision.

3

Laboratory tests the batch

Purity, moisture and contamination are recorded. PASS moves the batch on; FAIL rejects it.

4

Processor records each stage

Cleaning, drying, grinding and more, each with date, place and status.

5

Manufacturer creates the formulation

Processed, lab-approved batches are combined into a product with packaging details and a QR code.

6

Anyone verifies by QR

Consumers and regulators open the public page, with no login, and see the origin map, test results, timeline and ledger status.

What the system gives you

Geo-tagged collection

Collections are tested against configured regions, never accepted blindly.

Laboratory gate

Only PASS batches reach processing and manufacturing.

Role-based dashboards

Admin, collector, laboratory, processor and manufacturer each see only their own tools.

Tamper-evident ledger

SHA-256 hash links expose edited, reordered or removed records.

QR verification

One public page per product with the complete traceability timeline.

Why traceability matters

Herbal supply chains are long and the raw material changes hands many times. Without records it is hard to answer basic questions.

  • Was this herb collected somewhere it is allowed to be collected?
  • Was it tested before it was processed?
  • Which batches went into this exact product?
  • Has any record been changed since it was written?

Sustainability and safety

Recording the source type, collection method, permit and sustainability notes for every batch helps protect wild plant populations and gives regulators something concrete to inspect.

Laboratory results are attached to the same batch that ends up on the label, so a safety question can be traced back to the harvest that caused it.

A hash-linked ledger, kept honest about what it proves

Every event is stored with the SHA-256 hash of the event before it. Change any stored record and its hash no longer matches; delete one from the middle and the chain no longer links. An administrator can re-run the check at any time, and the public page shows a warning if it fails.

This is application-level record integrity and modification detection. It is not a decentralised network, and it cannot prove that a physical sack of herbs matches its record.
